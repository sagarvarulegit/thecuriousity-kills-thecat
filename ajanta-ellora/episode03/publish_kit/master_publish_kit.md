### Video Description

Thirty-three meters below the mountain ridge of Ellora, ancient stonecutters isolated a colossal rectangular island of volcanic basalt in a 100-foot abyss. Then began the most perilous subtractive excavation in human history: carving the sacred temple from the roof down.

While medieval Marathi folklore in the Katha-Kalpataru recounts Queen Manikavati's desperate vow and master builder Kokasa's promise to reveal the temple spire within days, modern archaeology reveals that top-down excavation was a cold engineering necessity. Without ground scaffolding, the mountain itself became their platform.

Join us on Episode 3 of our 10-part documentary webseries as we reconstruct the astronomical gnomon surveys, the geometry of the Vastu-Purusha Mandala, and the terrifying non-destructive acoustic sounding used to hollow out the inner sanctum without collapsing the overhead mountain.
