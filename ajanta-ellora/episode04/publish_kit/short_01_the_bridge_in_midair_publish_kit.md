## Description
Stand beneath Kailasa Temple at Ellora and look up. Suspended thirty feet above the courtyard floor, stone bridges connect giant pavilions. Look closer at the joints: there are no mortar lines. No wooden beams carry the span. No keystones lock an arch into place. These bridges were not assembled by masons. They were carved directly from the basalt mountain itself. Ancient rockcutters started from the mountaintop, trenching downward into the abyss. To create this bridge, they had to remove thousands of tons of rock from above, beside, and below it, without letting the span fracture. One wrong chisel strike, and forty tons of suspended rock would collapse into the courtyard. How did eighth-century engineers calculate the limits of living basalt? Watch Episode 4 to see how the mountain was turned into a suspended bridge.
