    Description

    Did you know one of Kailasa Temple's greatest architectural marvels is completely missing? Today, visitors marvel at surviving stone bridges linking the gateway, Nandi pavilion, and main hall. They seem to float in mid-air, carved from living rock. Yet if you look across to the southern cliff wall, you will see a sudden, jagged break. Centuries ago, another stone bridge spanned the gap toward the southern scarp. At some point in history, that massive rock span collapsed. Did an ancient earthquake shatter the unreinforced cantilever? Or did water seepage slowly weaken the basalt joint until gravity claimed it? The broken fracture scar is still clearly visible on the cliff today. It is a sobering reminder: even a mountain carved by masters is vulnerable to time. Explore the full forensic reconstruction in Episode 4: The Floating Bridges of Kailasa.
